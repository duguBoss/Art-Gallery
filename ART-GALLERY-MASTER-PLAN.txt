ART GALLERY
MASTER PRODUCT / KNOWLEDGE ARCHITECTURE
Version 1.0
Design Baseline for Gemini / Codex

============================================================
0. DOCUMENT PURPOSE
============================================================

This document is the master product specification for Art Gallery.

It is not a simple visual redesign brief.
It is not a request to make a generic "beautiful art website".
It is not a course platform.
It is not a paid knowledge platform.

Art Gallery is intended to become:

    A living, expandable atlas of human visual culture.

The long-term goal is to let users experience the richness, diversity,
history, relationships, techniques, ideas, and creative achievements
of humanity through an open, free, visual knowledge system.

This document must be treated as the primary product and design
baseline by Gemini, Codex, or any other AI working on the project.

When implementing future features, do NOT reinterpret the product from
scratch. Extend the existing architecture.

Core principle:

    Content determines presentation.
    Presentation must never limit the knowledge model.

The system must be designed so that adding architecture, sculpture,
photography, fashion, industrial design, film, animation, games,
digital art, generative art, AI art, crafts, new media, etc. does not
require redesigning the underlying product architecture.

============================================================
1. PRODUCT VISION
============================================================

Art Gallery should evolve from an art gallery into a comprehensive
visual knowledge universe.

The experience should communicate:

    "Humanity has created an astonishingly rich world."

Users should be able to:

    Explore
    Learn
    Discover
    Compare
    Connect ideas
    Study works
    Understand history
    Understand visual language
    Practice creative skills
    Find creative tools
    Discover products

The project should feel closer to:

    museum + encyclopedia + atlas + archive + visual laboratory
    + learning system + creative toolbox

It should NOT feel like:

    dashboard + SaaS admin panel + online course marketplace
    + generic card grid + AI-generated landing page.

============================================================
2. CORE PHILOSOPHY
============================================================

2.1 Knowledge is free

The project explicitly rejects the idea of putting knowledge itself
behind a payment wall.

Knowledge content should be openly accessible.

Examples of content that should remain free:

    Art history
    Design theory
    Visual language
    Color theory
    Composition
    Typography
    Architecture knowledge
    Sculpture knowledge
    Photography knowledge
    Film knowledge
    Animation knowledge
    3D concepts
    Motion concepts
    AI visual creation knowledge
    Techniques
    Historical context
    Artist information
    Style analysis
    Case studies
    Learning paths
    Practice exercises
    Cross-disciplinary relationships

Do not create:

    "Premium knowledge"
    "Locked chapters"
    "Paid advanced theory"
    "Pay to unlock knowledge"
    "Knowledge membership wall"
    "Paid course required to learn"

2.2 Knowledge belongs to humanity

The product philosophy should communicate that knowledge is a shared
cultural inheritance built across generations.

The project should encourage:

    learning
    sharing
    attribution
    open access
    cultural curiosity
    responsible reuse
    continued contribution.

2.3 What can be paid for

Users may pay for products, tools, materials, services, or labor.

Examples:

    templates
    design files
    creator kits
    Prompt packs
    3D assets
    materials
    presets
    workflows
    Figma resources
    PSD files
    AE resources
    motion assets
    creator utilities
    physical products
    software/tools
    commissioned services

The distinction is:

    Knowledge = free
    Product = paid when appropriate
    Support = donation
    Advertising = optional project revenue
    Sponsorship = optional project revenue

============================================================
3. COMMERCIAL MODEL
============================================================

Commercialization must never compromise the free knowledge principle.

Primary revenue layers:

    1. Creator Products
    2. Donations
    3. Sponsorships
    4. Google AdSense / contextual advertising

Recommended priority:

    Products > Donations > Sponsorships > Ads

Advertising is supplemental, not the identity of the site.

3.1 Products

Products should help people create faster or better.

Product pages should clearly communicate:

    what the product is
    what files/materials are included
    what problem it solves
    preview
    compatibility
    license
    price
    purchase action

Never imply:

    "Buy this to learn."

Instead:

    "The knowledge is free. This product saves creation time."

3.2 Donations

Donation messaging should emphasize maintaining free knowledge.

Suggested concept:

    KEEP KNOWLEDGE FREE

Possible explanation:

    "If Art Gallery has helped you, you can support the project and
    help us keep knowledge open, maintain the site, develop free tools,
    and continue expanding the archive."

Donation is not a purchase of knowledge.

3.3 Sponsorship

Sponsors may appear as tasteful editorial acknowledgements.

Examples:

    Supported by...
    Exhibition partner
    Creative technology partner
    Project sponsor

Do not make sponsorship visually overpower the content.

3.4 Google Ads

Reserve technically valid ad areas from the beginning.

Recommended placements:

    between long editorial sections
    search results
    selected content pages
    lower sections
    mobile-safe placements

Do NOT place intrusive ads:

    in the primary hero
    over important artwork
    inside critical learning steps
    as fake navigation
    as fake content
    through aggressive popups
    through deceptive click targets

The design system should allow ads to be enabled, disabled, or changed
without restructuring the page.

11. DATA / PRESENTATION SEPARATION
============================================================

This is a mandatory architecture principle.

Knowledge data and visual presentation must be independent.

Conceptually:

    KNOWLEDGE DATA
          |
          +-- Timeline View
          +-- Gallery View
          +-- Map View
          +-- Network View
          +-- Comparison View
          +-- Story View
          +-- Archive View
          +-- Interactive View
          +-- Learning View

different contexts.

============================================================
14. LEARNING SYSTEM
============================================================

Learning is free.

The system should teach through:

    Learn
    Observe
    Analyze
    Try
    Create
    Connect

Instead of merely presenting facts.

A lesson should preferably contain:

    Context
    Explanation
    Visual example
    Guided observation
    Key principles
    Practice
    Connections
    Further exploration

Example:

    Learn Composition
        -> See an artwork
        -> Identify focal point
        -> Identify balance
        -> Compare alternatives
        -> Try a constrained exercise
        -> Explore related artists

Do not turn the system into a conventional online course UI.

Avoid:

    course cards
    lesson progress dashboards
    locked lessons
    gamified points everywhere
    artificial certification

Learning should feel like exploring a museum, archive, studio, and
book simultaneously.

============================================================
19. ANTI-STIFFNESS DESIGN RULES
============================================================

These rules are mandatory.

DO NOT:

    make every section a card
    use identical 3-column grids everywhere
    use a sidebar for everything
    make every component rounded
    add shadows to every container
    use badges everywhere
    use excessive pills
    turn the site into a dashboard
    use giant gradient blobs without purpose
    use generic "AI premium" visual effects
    repeat identical section structures
    fill empty space with meaningless UI
    add controls merely because they look sophisticated
    put a CTA in every section
    make every page visually identical

DO:

    use editorial layouts
    use full-bleed imagery when appropriate
    use intentional whitespace
    use asymmetry where useful
    use strong typography
    use visual storytelling
    use image-led navigation
    use horizontal exploration when appropriate
    use timelines for historical information
    use maps for geographic information
    use graphs for relationships
    use 3D for spatial objects
    use annotations for visual analysis
    use comparison views for differences
    use long-form editorial layouts for deep reading

Key rule:

    Consistency belongs to the design system.
    Variety belongs to the content.

The graph should not be forced onto every page.
Use it when it improves understanding.

============================================================
24. CONTENT QUALITY
============================================================

Content should be:

    accurate
    sourced
    contextualized
    readable
    visual
    connected
    non-repetitive

Avoid shallow AI-generated encyclopedia text.

Each important knowledge item should preferably answer:

    What is it?
    Why did it appear?
    What problem / idea did it respond to?
    What makes it visually distinctive?
    Who developed it?
    What influenced it?
    What did it influence?
    Where did it happen?
    When did it happen?
    What materials / techniques are involved?
    Where can we see it today?
    How does it connect to modern creative practice?

Sources should be represented in the data model.

============================================================
25. SEO / GEO
============================================================

Every important entity should be independently understandable.

Pages should have:

    unique title
    concise description
    canonical URL
    semantic headings
    structured metadata
    internal links
    related entities
    source information
    language metadata

Content should be written so both traditional search engines and
AI answer systems can understand the entity and its relationships.

Do not keyword-stuff.

Knowledge relationships should create natural internal linking.

============================================================
26. MULTILINGUAL
============================================================

The system should support multiple languages from the architecture
level.

Language should be data-aware, not hard-coded into UI components.

Default language may be selected based on locale when possible, with
manual language switching always available.

Do not duplicate the entire application for each language.

Shared entities should have localized fields.

    progressive disclosure

Horizontal exploration may be used intentionally.

Avoid tiny metadata and dense desktop-style grids.

40. AI IMPLEMENTATION RULES
============================================================

These rules are mandatory for Gemini / Codex.

1. Do not redesign the product from scratch.

2. Read this document before modifying architecture.

3. Preserve useful existing content.

4. Do not remove the learning system merely to simplify UI.

5. Do not introduce paid knowledge.

6. Do not introduce a course marketplace.

7. Do not introduce a knowledge membership wall.

8. Do not add an admin panel unless explicitly requested.

9. Do not make every page use the same layout.

10. Do not turn everything into cards.

11. Do not invent UI simply to fill empty space.

12. Do not use dark/black styling merely because of the Art Gallery
    name or any assumed "premium" aesthetic.

13. Do not default to cyberpunk, neon, glassmorphism, or generic AI
    aesthetics.

14. Let the subject matter determine visual treatment.

15. Prefer real visual hierarchy over decorative effects.

16. Keep knowledge data independent from presentation components.

17. When adding a new domain, integrate it into the existing entity
    and relationship model.

18. Before adding a new component, check whether an existing
    presentation mode can solve the problem.

19. Before changing navigation, check the entire information
    architecture.

20. Before deleting content, verify whether it is part of the
    knowledge model.

21. Mobile must be considered from the beginning.

22. Accessibility must be preserved.

23. Performance matters because the site is image-heavy.

24. Sources and attribution must remain available.

25. If uncertain, prefer the option that increases long-term
    extensibility rather than short-term visual convenience.

41. DESIGN DECISION TEST
============================================================

Before implementing any major feature, ask:

    Does this help users understand something?
    Does this help users discover something?
    Does this help users connect ideas?
    Does this improve visual appreciation?
    Does this improve learning?
    Does this help creation?
    Does this preserve openness?
    Does this remain extensible?

If the answer is no to all meaningful questions, do not add it.

============================================================
42. CONTENT VS UI RULE
============================================================

Never force knowledge into a predefined UI.

Instead:

    Identify the nature of the knowledge.
    Select the appropriate narrative structure.
    Select the appropriate exhibition mode.
    Then design the interface.

Examples:

    History -> Timeline
    Geography -> Map
    Influence -> Network
    Sculpture -> 3D / multi-view
    Architecture -> plan / section / spatial sequence
    Painting -> image / detail / annotation
    Technique -> Process
    Material -> Material view
    Style comparison -> Comparison
    Biography -> Story
    Deep theory -> Editorial reading
